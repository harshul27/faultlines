## Notes per test

* **Fault lines: fragility predicts progression lost when a player goes off** — Each pair: same match, same first hour, player removed at 60'. Outcome: progressive passes 60-95' lost. *Scope:* The measure finds structurally important players in a passing system with no planted answer. Real football adds tactics, substitutes and opponents adapting: needs the injury/red-card study.
* **Fault lines: beyond position** — Predictor and outcome centred by position, so 'midfielders matter more' does not count. *Scope:* Same as above.
* **Fault lines: better than counting passes** — If a plain pass count predicts as well, fragility's extra complexity is not earning its place. *Scope:* Same as above.
* **Plan fit: robust to the exact weights** — Every weight multiplied by a random 0.5-1.5. A low tau would mean the ranking is mostly my weight choices. *Scope:* Holds for any data: this is a property of the scoring, not of football.
* **Plan fit: early reads hold up** — Checks that the minutes adjustment keeps early verdicts from flipping later. *Scope:* Real players vary more between halves (tactics, fatigue, game state), so expect lower agreement on real data.
* **Plan fit: reliable within a match** — A score that changes completely between halves is measuring noise, not the player. *Scope:* In synthetic matches players have no lasting individual quality beyond position and team strength, so this is a floor; real players should be more consistent.
* **Flags: not too many, not too few** — Too many flags and coaches stop reading them. *Scope:* Rates will shift on real data; retune the 1.5x / 0.6x / 12-point thresholds there.
* **Flags: 'fading' predicts the next 15 minutes** — Next-15 fit regressed on current fit + the flag. Without the flag adding information, it is regression to the mean dressed up as insight. *Scope:* The generator has no fatigue, so in neutral matches nothing real can fade; the storyline match (man-marking from 60') is the only true decline. Real test: tracking data (high-speed running) and coach substitutions.
* **Key-moment cues: informative, not spammy** — Counts fault-line shifts, alerts and narrowing per match. *Scope:* Precision (are they the moments an analyst would pick?) needs analyst labels on real matches.
* **Expected points added tracks results** — Sanity check that the replacement for 'clutch points' measures contribution to results. *Scope:* By construction it inherits xG quality; on real data use the provider's xG.
* **Plan reader (keyword rules)** — Negations, opponent instructions, sheets, other languages, emphasis. *Scope:* Language understanding: independent of the match data.
